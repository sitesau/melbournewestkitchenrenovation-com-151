Understanding the Written Quote

Every project starts with a free in home measure up and an itemised fixed scope written quote. That makes the quote more than a price tag. It is the main document for checking what has actually been allowed for before work starts. Compare whether demolition, rough in work, cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, plumbing, electrical, appliances and finishing are included. The site also says drawings are shown before cabinetry is made. That sequence is important because changes on paper are easier to manage than changes after fabrication begins. Ask which items are fixed in the scope and which would be treated as variations. Check whether project management is included from design to handover. Make sure the contract explains payment stages and the process for approving changes.

Timeline and Fabrication Risks

The published timeline is useful because it splits the job into real stages. A standard full renovation usually runs around 6 to 12 weeks from contract to handover. Cabinetry and benchtop fabrication often takes 4 to 6 weeks, then the on site stage usually takes 2 to 4 weeks for demolition, install, plumbing, electrical and finishing. A makeover is generally quicker. That means the biggest delays are not always on site. The more practical risk is starting fabrication before layout, materials and appliance choices are settled. If you want the program to stay on track, ask when selections must be locked in, when templates are taken for benchtops and what would restart fabrication or installation timing. The handover standard is also stated clearly, cleanup, a working kitchen, and warranty and compliance paperwork in writing.

Benchtop Compliance and Materials

Benchtop decisions now involve a clear compliance check. The business states that engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. It says current options include porcelain and sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber and compliant low silica alternatives. That makes early selection more useful than late comparison. Ask to see the benchtop choices that fit your layout, sink plan and finish level, then price them side by side. If you are weighing appearance against a simpler program, it helps to decide that before cabinetry is finalised. The site also says projects run with the right permits and registered plumbing and electrical trades in line with Victorian building requirements. If the layout changes or the work goes beyond a surface update, ask what approvals may need to be checked before demolition is booked.
